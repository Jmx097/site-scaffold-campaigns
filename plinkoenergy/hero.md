# Hero — plinkoenergy.com

## Intent

Establish credibility of both brands within one scroll — Plinko as the sourcing firm, VersaFile as the delivery partner. Mid-market buyer lands skeptical of "partnership" claims. The hero has to make the division of labor legible fast. Voice: peer-to-peer Canadian, warmer than enterprise, more pragmatic than MSP.

## Critique

- **Headline "Plinko Energy"** is the brand name, not a value proposition. Mid-market buyers skim — the H1 should carry more weight. Consider: "Document management for mid-market Canadian utilities" as H1, brand mark above as logo/wordmark.
- **Subheadline names both brands and the division of labor.** Good.
- **"Regional utilities, IPPs, and energy co-ops"** — names three specific buyer segments. Strong opening for the buyer to self-identify. Keep.
- **"Big-four consulting engagement" as the negative reference** — specific, recognizable, doesn't trash-talk a named competitor. Good.
- **"One call, two specialists"** — concise, differentiated. Keep.
- **Risk-reversal matches cta-rules.md** ("Worst case, one honest opinion on the M-Files question"). Good.
- **Button label matches cta-rules.md** ("Book a 15-minute intro call"). Good.
- **No regulator mention in hero.** Sitemap intent says regulator names should appear at least once on the page — could land in the pain section, doesn't have to be in hero. Acceptable.
- **"Actually do the work"** is a nice piece of peer-to-peer phrasing — signals we're not just another "advisory-only" firm. Keep.
- **Voice check:** Warmer than versafilecanada hero, which is correct per voice-and-guardrails.md. Not as casual as tryplinko. Good placement on the scale.
- **Brand hierarchy:** Plinko name appears first (in header/logo), VersaFile name appears in subheadline. Matches positioning.md rules.

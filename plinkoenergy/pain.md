# Pain — plinkoenergy.com

## Intent

Name the mid-market gap — priced out of big-four, too small for enterprise ECM vendor attention, self-serving on SharePoint and knowing it. Buyer thinks "Does this apply to me? I'm not enterprise, I'm not tiny." Leaves thinking "They're talking to my size."

---

## Draft (V1 adapted)

> **The mid-market document management gap**
>
> Mid-market utilities sit on document systems built for a different regulatory load than today's. OEB filings. Engineering record retention. NERC audit readiness. Capital project lifecycle documents that outgrew SharePoint three capital budgets ago.
>
> The options on the table usually look like:
>
> - Big-four consulting firms that price mid-market buyers out before discovery ever starts.
> - Enterprise ECM vendors that sell a platform and vanish at go-live.
> - Internal teams trying to hold it together with SharePoint, naming conventions, and determination.
>
> None of those match the scale of the problem at a 100-1,500 person utility.

---

## Critique

- **"Mid-market document management gap"** — direct, specific. Good H2.
- **First paragraph names three concrete document categories** (OEB filings, engineering retention, NERC audit, capital project lifecycle). Structural proof that we know the buyer's day. Keep.
- **"Three capital budgets ago"** — peer-to-peer phrasing, funny-but-true. Keep.
- **Three-option list with honest limitations of each.** Matches the voice-and-guardrails "acknowledgment of trade-offs" pattern. Keep.
- **"Hold it together with SharePoint, naming conventions, and determination"** — respectful, honest, slightly wry. Keep.
- **"None of those match the scale of the problem at a 100-1,500 person utility"** — explicit company-size grounding. Matches brief ICP. Keep.
- **Regulator mentions:** OEB once, NERC once in first paragraph. Within the "twice per section max" rule.
- **Length is tight** — 4 elements (paragraph + 3-bullet list + closing). Scannable. Good for mid-market buyer skim.
- **No voice-and-guardrails violations.**
- **Consider:** Is "Big-four consulting" too pejorative? It's accurate — big-four IS too expensive for mid-market. But some buyers have a big-four relationship already and may bristle. Could soften to "Global consulting engagements priced for enterprise budgets." Default: keep the direct version; it reads more honest.

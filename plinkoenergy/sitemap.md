## Per-section intent

### 1. Hero

**Job:** Establish credibility of BOTH brands within one scroll — Plinko as the sourcing firm, VersaFile as the delivery partner.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Skeptical of "partnership" claims. Has seen channel-partner theater. Wants to know which party actually does what.

**Buyer state after this section:** "OK, two companies, clear division of labor. Scroll further."

**Must include:**
- Headline that names both brands or at least their relationship
- Subheadline that clarifies the offer: mid-market Canadian utilities, M-Files delivered by VersaFile, conversation sourced by Plinko
- One primary CTA + risk-reversal ("Worst case, one honest opinion on the M-Files question")

**Must not include:**
- Agency-speak about partnerships
- "We collaborate closely with..." mush
- Enterprise-style opening ("VersaFile, a Canadian M-Files partner for energy...") — too formal for mid-market voice

---

### 2. Pain

**Job:** Name the mid-market gap — priced out of big-four, too small for enterprise ECM vendor attention, self-serving on SharePoint and knowing it.

**Buyer state on arrival:** "Does this apply to me? I'm not enterprise, I'm not tiny."

**Buyer state after this section:** "They're talking to my size."

**Must include:**
- Acknowledgment that big-four consulting won't call back (or is too expensive when it does)
- Acknowledgment that SharePoint + file shares is the default and it's surviving but not thriving
- Reference to regulator-aware operations (OEB, CER, NERC CIP) — mid-market utilities are regulator-aware even if their doc systems aren't

**Must not include:**
- Patronizing tone ("mid-market companies struggle with...")
- Enterprise pain framing (audits at multinational scale) — wrong buyer
- MSP-adjacent casualness — this is still an energy sector buyer

---

### 3. Solution

**Job:** Explain the partnership mechanic so the buyer understands why two firms are on one call and why that's better than a traditional RFP shortlist.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Needs to understand the logistics. "Is this one contract or two? Who's my point of contact?"

**Buyer state after this section:** "Clear: Plinko sources and manages the intro, VersaFile does the implementation. One engagement, two specialists, no shortlist dance."

**Must include:**
- Plinko's role: sourcing, qualifying, structured intro, not a cold sales pitch
- VersaFile's role: the implementation (configure, deploy, train, stay)
- The combined benefit: no six-vendor shortlist, no "why are we talking to four consultants"
- Brief acknowledgment that this is how it actually works — not a marketing construct

**Must not include:**
- Full Plinko outbound stack detail (wrong buyer for Apollo/HeyReach name-drops)
- Any MSP framing
- Extended VersaFile capability grid (that's versafilecanada.com's job; keep it tight here)

---

### 4. Process

**Job:** Describe what the first call + decision looks like, so the buyer can picture the next 30 days.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Considering the call. Wants to know what happens if they book and what happens after.

**Buyer state after this section:** "If I book, I get a call with the right people, a structured recommendation, and no pressure to sign anything."

**Must include:**
- The call itself: 15 minutes, Plinko and VersaFile both on, structured agenda
- What happens after a qualified call: VersaFile scopes, Plinko steps back
- What happens if it's not a fit: honest "no, here's who to look at" (reinforces the risk-reversal)

**Must not include:**
- Enterprise-style sales-cycle descriptions
- A detailed deployment timeline (that's for after the call, not on the page)

---

### 5. FAQ

**Job:** Answer objections that specifically come up at mid-market scale and under a partnership structure.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Scanning for disqualifiers before they book.

**Questions to answer:**
1. Is M-Files overkill for a utility our size?
2. Why are two companies on the call?
3. Who do we contract with — Plinko or VersaFile?
4. How is this different from a big-four consulting engagement?
5. What if we already use SharePoint — can M-Files work alongside it?
6. What does "implementation partner" actually mean?

**Must not include:**
- Pricing
- Customer names without approval
- Trash-talk of other partners or consultants

---

### 6. CTA

**Job:** Close with the partnership risk-reversal: "Worst case, one honest opinion."

**Must include:**
- Final CTA headline
- Risk-reversal sentence
- Cal.com button + form
- Both brand footers

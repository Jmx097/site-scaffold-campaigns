# Solution — plinkoenergy.com

## Intent

Explain the partnership mechanic so the buyer understands why two firms are on one call and why that's better than a traditional RFP shortlist. Plinko's role: sourcing, qualifying, structured intro. VersaFile's role: implementation (configure, deploy, train, stay). No agency mush.

---

## Draft (V1 adapted)

> **How this works**
>
> **Plinko runs the outbound engine.** We source and qualify conversations with decision-makers at mid-market Canadian utilities and IPPs who are actively feeling records, compliance, or asset-document pain. We're the sourcing specialists — when a conversation warrants real delivery, we bring VersaFile in.
>
> **VersaFile delivers the M-Files work.** They're Canada's M-Files implementation partner for the electric power, utilities, and midstream energy sectors. Transmission and distribution utilities, IPPs, midstream operators — VersaFile has configured, deployed, and stayed engaged across the Canadian energy space. Plinko surfaces the opportunity. VersaFile delivers the solution.
>
> **You get one call, two specialists.** No six-vendor RFP shortlist. No "why are we talking to four consultants." When you book the intro, both sides are on the call.

---

## Critique

- **Three clear blocks** — Plinko's role, VersaFile's role, combined benefit. Matches sitemap intent.
- **"Sourcing specialists"** — strong positioning for Plinko in this context (without dragging MSP-stack names into an energy-sector page).
- **"Canada's M-Files implementation partner for the electric power, utilities, and midstream energy sectors"** — exact language from positioning.md. Keep.
- **"Configured, deployed, and stayed engaged"** — shorthand for the four-capability model used in full on versafilecanada.com. Appropriate here (briefer for a different buyer). Keep.
- **"One call, two specialists"** — repeats the hero's phrase. Good reinforcement in close position.
- **"No six-vendor RFP shortlist. No 'why are we talking to four consultants.'"** — two negative framings that build trust by naming a pain the buyer has lived. Keep.
- **Voice check:** Warmer than versafilecanada's "Solution," less casual than tryplinko's. Contractions present ("we're," "they've") — appropriate.
- **Brand hierarchy:** Plinko leads, VersaFile credited clearly. Matches positioning.md rules.
- **Agency-speak check:** "Runs the outbound engine" could read agency-ish, but "outbound engine" is a legitimate industry term and Plinko literally runs one. Acceptable.
- **"When you book the intro, both sides are on the call"** — concrete expectation-setting. Helps the buyer picture the logistics.
- **Consider:** Does this section need one more line on what the buyer owns vs. what each party delivers, post-call? Or does that belong in process.md? Default: keep this section focused on the "why two firms together" question; let process.md handle the "what happens next" question.

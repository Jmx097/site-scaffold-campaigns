# Positioning — The Four Motions

The narrative foundation for all four domains. When a section file or an HTML page asks "what are we, and why?", the answer lives here. Per-page briefs reference this; they don't reinvent it.

---

## The umbrella story

Jon operates two adjacent businesses that share a technical backbone:

**VersaFile** — enterprise content management consulting; reseller of M-Files for regulated industries in Canada (primarily energy/utilities).

**Plinko Solutions** — a done-for-you outbound + AI-automation agency for MSPs. Plinko was built to run its own outbound on a stack of Apollo + Hunter + HeyReach + Smartlead + n8n, orchestrated by OpenClaw and Claude Cowork. Plinko sells that same capability to other MSPs — both as pipeline generation and as internal AI automation.

The two businesses intersect at **Plinko Energy** — a partnership motion where Plinko's outbound engine sources conversations with mid-market Canadian utilities, and VersaFile delivers the M-Files implementation.

---

## The four motions (one per domain)

### Motion 1 — VersaFile Direct

**Domain:** `versafilecanada.com`

**Buyer:** Records/IM managers, compliance leads, IT directors, engineering document control owners, and VPs of operations at Canadian electric utilities, crown corporations, IPPs, midstream energy operators, and district energy companies. Companies in the 1,500-10,000 employee band. Regulated under NERC, OEB, CER, or provincial equivalents.

**Core promise:** *Canada's M-Files implementation partner for the energy sector. We've done this. We know your regulator. We configure, deploy, and stay.*

**Differentiation:**
- Canadian — matters because US vendors don't know OEB, CER, or provincial regulatory context
- Sector-specialized — not a generalist M-Files partner
- Implementation-deep, not reseller-shallow — deploys, configures, trains, supports

**Competitive frame:**
- Not big-four consulting (too expensive, too generalist)
- Not global M-Files resellers (not Canadian context)
- Not internal SharePoint duct-tape (doesn't scale to regulator)

**Risk reversal:** 15-min call, no pressure. Enterprise buyers expect low-friction first steps.

**Tone pole:** Most formal. "Serious."

---

### Motion 2 — Plinko Energy (Plinko + VersaFile combined)

**Domain:** `plinkoenergy.com`

**Buyer:** Same roles as Motion 1, but at **100-1,500 employee** utilities, IPPs, energy co-ops, First Nations utilities, and district energy operators. Priced out of big-four consulting. Too small for enterprise ECM vendor attention. Self-serving on SharePoint and knowing it.

**Core promise:** *Document management conversations for mid-market Canadian utilities — sourced by Plinko, delivered by VersaFile. One call, two specialists.*

**Differentiation:**
- Plinko surfaces the right conversation (most mid-market utilities aren't on anyone's cold-call list)
- VersaFile delivers the solution (implementation partner with sector credibility)
- Combined: no six-vendor shortlist, no "why are we talking to four consultants"

**Competitive frame:**
- Not big-four consulting (out of budget)
- Not a lone reseller running a 90-day demo (not trusted)
- Not the internal team's SharePoint-and-hope (they already know it's broken)

**Risk reversal:** 15-min call. Worst case, one opinion on the M-Files question.

**Tone pole:** Warmer than VersaFile Direct. Peer-to-peer but professional. Canadian, mid-market, pragmatic.

**Sensitive dependency:** Requires VersaFile leadership sign-off before the page goes live. Co-branding is not automatic.

---

### Motion 3 — Plinko MSP (low-commitment entry)

**Domain:** `tryplinko.com`

**Buyer:** Founder/owner of a 10-50 person MSP. Stuck between "too small for a sales hire" and "growing too fast to do outbound between support tickets." Has probably tried cold email once, tried Upwork leads once, tried "I'll do 30 min of LinkedIn daily" and dropped it.

**Core promise:** *A complete outbound engine for MSPs that can't justify a full BDR team. Book 2x more qualified conversations without hiring, training, or managing another human.*

**Differentiation:**
- Not a lead list — we source and engage signal-triggered accounts
- Not a tool stack — a managed routine, the MSP sees results not tools
- Stack transparency — Apollo, Hunter, HeyReach, Smartlead, OpenClaw + n8n, named explicitly to build trust

**Competitive frame:**
- Not buying Upwork lists (burns domain, no fit)
- Not hiring a first BDR (expensive, slow ramp, hard to manage)
- Not running from Gmail primary domain (domain reputation suicide)

**Risk reversal:** 15-min call, low-pressure. No pitch deck, no mandatory demo.

**Tone pole:** Peer-to-peer, casual but crisp. MSP-founder to MSP-founder.

---

### Motion 4 — Plinko MSP DFY (committed buyer + AI automation)

**Domain:** `dfyplinko.com`

**Buyer:** Same MSP as Motion 3, but further along. They've already decided they're not building outbound internally. They want the full engine running in 90 days. Increasingly they want AI-native internal ops too — automated ticket triage, client onboarding, doc generation — and they know Claude/OpenClaw is where that lives but they don't have time to become the specialist.

**Core promise:** *Done-for-you outbound engine and AI automation setup for MSPs. Powered by the same Claude Cowork and OpenClaw stack we run our own business on. 90 days to first qualified pipeline and your first internal automations live.*

**The combined offer explained:**
- **Outbound side:** pipeline, meetings on your calendar, everything tryplinko delivers but at the committed tier.
- **Internal automation side:** We use OpenClaw + Claude Cowork + n8n to set up your MSP's internal AI workflows — ticket triage, client onboarding docs, proposal generation, internal reporting. Whatever's eating your ops bandwidth, we automate with the same stack we used to build the outbound engine.
- **The honest proof moment:** "The engine we sold you runs on the same infrastructure we use for our own pipeline. Our landing pages are built with it. Our weekly outbound routine runs on it. We drink our own champagne."

**Differentiation:**
- Most AI consultants stop at "here's a ChatGPT prompt." We ship running systems.
- Most outbound agencies know nothing about AI. We build both on the same foundation.
- Most DFY offers are a CSV and an invoice. Ours is an infrastructure you can audit.

**Competitive frame:**
- Not a generic AI consultancy (they don't know MSPs)
- Not a generic outbound agency (they don't touch internal ops)
- Not a junior offshore "VA + GPT" setup (no depth, no stack)
- Not DIY Claude Cowork (MSP owners don't have 40 hours to learn the platform)

**Risk reversal:** 90-day commitment, month-to-month after. Explicit anti-ICP filter on the page ("not for you if…"). Weekly reporting so the MSP can kill it if it's not working.

**Tone pole:** Most assertive of the four. Confident, committed, specific. Not arrogant — specific.

**AI-search target queries (per the strategic decision):**
- "Done-for-you Claude Cowork setup"
- "OpenClaw for MSPs" / "Claude Cowork for MSPs"
- "AI automation agency for MSPs"

The page must make those entity phrases prominent (in headings, in FAQ Q&A, in schema metadata). Semantic relevance requires the words to appear in context, not just meta tags.

## Brand hierarchy rules

**On versafilecanada.com:** VersaFile brand only. No Plinko reference. Plinko does not appear on this page — it's an enterprise anchor motion and Plinko's frame would dilute credibility.

**On plinkoenergy.com:** Dual branding. Plinko leads (sourcing). VersaFile is explicitly credited for implementation. Footer names both. Requires leadership sign-off from VersaFile before launch.

**On tryplinko.com:** Plinko brand only. No VersaFile mention — this is the MSP motion, VersaFile has no relevance.

**On dfyplinko.com:** Plinko brand only. Named tools (OpenClaw, Claude Cowork, Apollo, HeyReach, Smartlead, n8n) appear prominently because (a) trust-through-transparency and (b) AI-search ranking depends on named-entity density. No VersaFile mention.

## Why this positioning works (strategic rationale)

1. **Each domain owns one narrow claim.** We don't try to say everything on every page. Specificity earns trust.

2. **Proof isn't generic — it's structural.** Plinko runs its own outbound on the stack it sells. VersaFile has shipped M-Files in regulated Canadian energy. Each claim has a structural, not-stated-in-a-testimonial source of credibility.

3. **The four motions don't compete for the same buyer.** Enterprise utility ≠ mid-market utility ≠ MSP curious ≠ MSP committed. Each page does one job, cleanly.

4. **AI search is a non-obvious lever for dfyplinko.** Most agency landing pages are written for human cold-email recipients only. A page that's also semantically legible to Claude/GPT/Perplexity for queries like "OpenClaw for MSPs" is a category-owning play — near-zero competition, growing query volume, ICP alignment.

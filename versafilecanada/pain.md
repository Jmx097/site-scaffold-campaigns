# Pain — versafilecanada.com

## Intent

Name what records teams at Canadian utilities actually face so the buyer recognizes themselves and trusts we've been in the room. Concrete operational details — drawings control, regulatory audit windows, fragmented SharePoint + file shares, manual metadata — not "document chaos" hand-waving. Acknowledge what SharePoint is good at so we don't sound anti-Microsoft. Buyer walks away thinking "they've seen this."

---

## Draft (new, drawn from V1 "What We Do" + extended)

> **What your records team is actually dealing with**
>
> Folder hierarchies break the moment a document is needed in two business contexts at once. An engineering drawing is an asset record, a regulatory artifact, a maintenance reference, and a safety document — all at the same time. The current setup usually looks like some combination of SharePoint for collaboration, network shares for drawings, point tools for Bluebeam markups, and Excel spreadsheets tracking which version is authoritative.
>
> That works until a regulator opens an audit window. Or an engineering change request lands and the drawing of record is in three places with three different revision numbers. Or a long-tenured records manager retires and takes the convention knowledge with them.
>
> SharePoint is good at collaboration. It was never designed to produce regulator-ready evidence on demand, track revision lineage against an asset register, or enforce retention schedules across document classes with different legal and operational obligations.

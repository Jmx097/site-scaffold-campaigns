## Per-section intent

### 1. Hero

**Job:** Prove within one scroll that this is a serious Canadian M-Files partner who works at the reader's regulatory scale.

**Buyer state on arrival:** 8-12 seconds of attention. Skeptical. Phone or desktop. Came from a cold email or a Google search with intent keywords like "M-Files Canada" or "ECM utilities Canada."

**Buyer state after this section:** "OK, these people are actually in my market. Scroll further."

**Must include:**
- A headline naming the sector (electric utilities / energy) and the product (M-Files) and the location (Canada)
- A subheadline that names a regulatory body (NERC CIP, OEB, or CER) — structural proof element
- One primary CTA (Cal.com) + risk-reversal sentence
- No customer logos (unless published approval exists)

**Must not include:**
- Marketing superlatives
- Enthusiasm ("!!!")
- Plinko references
- Stock photos of businesspeople shaking hands

---

### 2. Pain

**Job:** Name what records teams at Canadian utilities actually face so the buyer recognizes themselves and trusts we've been in the room.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Intrigued but not sold. Looking for evidence we understand the day-to-day, not just the product category.

**Buyer state after this section:** "They've seen this. They know our problem."

**Must include:**
- Concrete operational details: drawings control, regulatory audit windows, engineering document review, fragmented SharePoint + file shares, manual metadata
- Named regulator or audit scenario (once — not overused)
- Acknowledgment of what SharePoint is good at (to avoid sounding anti-Microsoft) and what it isn't (the audit-evidence problem)

**Must not include:**
- Generic "document chaos" language
- Fear-mongering ("you could fail an audit!")
- Anything that lists problems without showing we've lived them

---

### 3. Solution

**Job:** Define VersaFile's deliverable in four capabilities — configure, deploy, train, stay — each specific enough that the buyer can imagine the engagement.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Wants to know what we actually do, not "we help with ECM."

**Buyer state after this section:** "These aren't four generic bullets. These are four specific capabilities with specific deliverables."

**Must include:**
- Four headers: Configure / Deploy / Train / Stay
- Under each: what it includes, what the buyer's team does vs. what we do, what the end state looks like
- At least one named framework or standard per capability (e.g., configure → metadata schema aligned to NERC CIP evidence categories)

**Must not include:**
- "We partner with you on your journey"
- Vague scope ("comprehensive support")
- More than four capabilities (the four-cap grid is the structural proof — don't dilute)

---

### 4. Process

**Job:** Describe what a real engagement looks like so the buyer can price time, effort, and ownership internally.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Starting to mentally evaluate. Wants to know: how long, how much of our team's time, what order.

**Buyer state after this section:** "I know roughly what this looks like. I can make a mental checklist for internal review."

**Must include:**
- Phases with rough time bounds ("Discovery: 2-3 weeks" etc.)
- Who does what: VersaFile vs. client team
- One line about what "stay" means post-deployment (the honest part — we don't ghost after go-live)

**Must not include:**
- Methodology trademarks ("our proprietary 7-step framework")
- Hand-waving on timelines ("every engagement is unique")
- Gantt-chart decoration

---

### 5. FAQ

**Job:** Answer the top objections a records manager or IT director raises internally when evaluating M-Files + VersaFile.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Doing due diligence. Likely scrolling this before a meeting.

**Buyer state after this section:** "Nothing disqualifying. Internal case holds together."

**Questions to answer (in this order):**
1. Why M-Files vs. SharePoint (or staying on file shares)?
2. Why VersaFile vs. a global M-Files reseller?
3. What does regulatory-audit-ready actually mean in an M-Files context?
4. How long does a typical deployment take?
5. What happens if our team can't dedicate 2-3 people full-time during deployment?
6. What's included in "stay" (post-deployment support)?

**Must not include:**
- Pricing (stays off the page)
- Customer names without approval
- Comparisons to named competitors (libel-adjacent risk; stay categorical)

---

### 6. CTA

**Job:** Close with a named, time-bounded next step and risk reversal.

**Buyer state on arrival:** Either ready to book, ready to fill the form, or scrolling once more before leaving.

**Buyer state after this section:** Clicked Cal.com, submitted the form, or left with the page's brand in mind for when the decision comes up internally.

**Must include:**
- Final CTA headline ("The next step")
- Risk-reversal sentence
- Cal.com button + contact form
- Direct email as a final backstop for buyers who won't click either

**Must not include:**
- Pressure language
- Multiple buttons to different destinations
- Newsletter signup

# Solution — versafilecanada.com

## Intent

Define VersaFile's deliverable in four capabilities — configure, deploy, train, stay — each specific enough that the buyer can imagine the engagement. Each capability gets what it includes, what the buyer's team does vs. what VersaFile does, and at least one named framework or standard. No "we partner with you on your journey." Not more than four — the four-cap grid is the structural proof.

---

## Draft (V1 "What We Do" expanded, plus new "Why M-Files" inline)

> **What we build**
>
> VersaFile is Canada's M-Files implementation partner for the electric power, utilities, and midstream energy sectors. The engagement has four phases — and we stay involved in all four.
>
> **Configure.** Metadata schema aligned to your regulator's evidence categories (NERC CIP document classes, OEB retention obligations, CER reporting requirements), not a generic ECM template. Object classes, workflows, and permissions mapped to how your records team, engineering team, and compliance team actually work.
>
> **Deploy.** M-Files stood up in your environment (cloud, on-prem, or hybrid), integrated where it matters — Microsoft 365, SharePoint, AutoCAD or Bentley document roots, maintenance systems, asset registers — without rip-and-replace. Migration from your existing shares happens in phases, not as a single risky cutover.
>
> **Train.** Role-based training. Records managers get the admin console. Engineering leads get drawing control workflows. Operations staff get one-click search and retrieval. Not a single 2-hour webinar — embedded training across the first 60 days, reinforced with reference material your team actually uses.
>
> **Stay.** We don't ship and ghost. A named delivery lead remains engaged post-go-live for quarterly reviews, configuration updates, new document classes as your business changes, and incident support if something breaks. Ongoing support tiered to your team's capacity — from full-service to lightweight quarterly check-ins.
>
> **Where we work:** engineering information management (drawing lifecycle, revision control, asset-record linkage), regulatory records and retention (automated filing, retention schedules, audit trails for NERC CIP, OEB, CER), capital project document control (EPC deliverables, handover documentation, commissioning records), and Microsoft 365 / SharePoint integration.

## Critique

- **Four capabilities, clearly delineated.** Good — matches sitemap intent.
- **Each capability names a framework or specific deliverable.** Configure cites NERC CIP, OEB, CER. Deploy names AutoCAD / Bentley. Train describes role-based phases. Stay names the delivery model. Good.
- **"We don't ship and ghost" is a tonally perfect line** — direct, slightly punchy, earned by the four-capability specificity above it. Keep.
- **"Rip-and-replace" is a common enterprise ECM term** — appropriate here.
- **"Where we work" list at the end duplicates some of the capability descriptions.** Decision point: is the four-cap block the engagement model, and the "where we work" list the functional footprint? If so, keep both but consider separating with a short transitional sentence.
- **No regulator overuse** (NERC, OEB, CER each appear once in the capability block, once in the footer list — within the "twice per section max" rule in voice-and-guardrails.md).
- **No marketing superlatives.** No "comprehensive" or "best-in-class."
- **Contractions used** ("We don't ship and ghost") — fine per voice rules, hero is the only zone that should avoid contractions.
- **Claim check:** "Migration from your existing shares happens in phases, not as a single risky cutover." Is this in proof-library.md? It's a description of how we work, which is essentially structural/Validated. Low risk but should be noted in proof library as a delivery convention.
- **Consider:** Is "embedded training across the first 60 days" a commitment or an approximation? If it's a commitment in the SOW, great; if it's a rough estimate, either pin it down or hedge it as "typical."
